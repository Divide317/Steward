# Game screen

Scope: the single play screen of Steward (`public/index.html`), every state of it: intro, play, question dialog, crisis, link cut and puzzle, drawers for log, agents, training and judging, stage-up and game-over sheets. Mode: Experience; the world leads.

Audience and job: casual browser players and portfolio reviewers, desktop and phone. They must grasp within a minute that they are the AI the colony asks, answer, and see what their words did.

Constraints: Krillion (krillion.io) is the owner's named visual blueprint, adapted rather than copied. Plain HTML, CSS and JS modules; canvas map; no build step; free hosting.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The colony is a pixel game board on a table and every settler's question is a card dealt into your hand. It refuses the sim-HUD arrangement of resource bar, side panel and map.

OWN-WORLD: Krillion's grammar: VT323 body, Silkscreen display, square frames made of a dark gap and a hard cyan ring, pixel art rendered pixelated, navy table #0d1830, ice text #e8f1ff, signal cyan #4de3ff, reach gold #ffd166, critical coral #ff5d8f. Cards carry a playing-card corner suit drawn as a pixel icon per topic.

STORY: You see a town on a board, a hand of cards from its people, and a score track of how many rely on you. You play a card, a piece walks off, the tile keeps a mark of what your answer did.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Pixel sky band across the top holding the STEWARD lettering, day and weather. The board, ring-framed, owns the centre; the reach score track runs round its frame in gold pips. Measures stand as tracks at the board's left on desktop, a strip under the sky on phones. The hand fans along the bottom edge; the most urgent card sits raised and largest. Compute tokens sit beside the hand.

FORM: The Pixel Tabletop, position 5 of 7 on my list, seed:fa4f572cfa2dc799. Raises: permanent tile marks (ebru), staged crisis event card with an after-image (raku), static only while the link is cut (film), board and track grow per stage (secession), most urgent card largest (Scher).

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
